# Encryption & Key Management Policy

**Effective date:** {{effective_date}}
**Policy owner:** {{security_officer}}

## Purpose

This policy sets the minimum cryptographic protections that {{org_name}} applies to its information so that data remains confidential and intact even if storage media, networks, or backups are exposed. It also establishes how encryption keys are managed across their lifecycle, because encryption is only as strong as the protection of its keys.

## Scope

This policy applies to:

- All production systems, databases, object storage, file shares, queues, and backups operated by or on behalf of {{org_name}}.
- All company-managed endpoints, including laptops, desktops, and mobile devices.
- All network connections that carry company or customer data, whether internal or external.
- All employees, contractors, and service providers who design, operate, or administer these systems.

## Roles and responsibilities

- **Security Officer ({{security_officer}})** owns this policy, approves cryptographic standards, approves exceptions, and reviews key management practices at least annually.
- **Engineering and infrastructure teams** implement encryption on the systems they build and operate and keep configuration evidence available for audit.
- **Key custodians** are named individuals authorized to administer key management systems. They follow the procedures in this policy and must never share key material through unapproved channels.
- **All personnel** must use only approved tools for storing and transmitting sensitive data and must report any suspected key compromise immediately.

## Policy statements

### Approved algorithms

1. Only industry-accepted, publicly reviewed algorithms may be used. Symmetric encryption must use AES with a key length of at least 128 bits, with AES-256 preferred for data at rest.
2. Asymmetric keys must be at least RSA 2048-bit or elliptic-curve keys of at least 256 bits.
3. Hashing for integrity must use SHA-256 or stronger. Passwords must be stored only with a salted, adaptive algorithm such as bcrypt, scrypt, or Argon2.
4. Deprecated algorithms and protocols, including DES, 3DES, RC4, MD5, SHA-1 for signatures, SSL, and TLS 1.0/1.1, must not be used.
5. Custom or home-grown cryptographic algorithms must not be used.

### Encryption at rest

1. All databases, object storage buckets, block volumes, file systems, and backups that store Internal, Confidential, or Restricted data must be encrypted at rest.
2. Cloud storage services must have default encryption enabled at the account or resource level so that new resources are encrypted automatically.
3. Restricted data, such as credentials, payment data, and special-category personal data, should additionally be protected with field-level or application-level encryption where technically feasible.
4. Company-managed endpoints must use full-disk encryption as described in the Asset Management Policy.
5. Removable media must not hold Confidential or Restricted data unless the media is encrypted.

### Encryption in transit

1. All data transmitted over public networks must be encrypted using TLS 1.2 or higher.
2. Public-facing web applications and APIs must redirect HTTP to HTTPS and should send HTTP Strict Transport Security headers.
3. Connections between internal services, and between applications and databases, must use TLS wherever the service supports it.
4. Administrative access to infrastructure must use encrypted protocols such as SSH, TLS, or a VPN. Telnet, plain FTP, and other cleartext protocols are prohibited.
5. TLS certificates must be issued by a trusted certificate authority for public endpoints, monitored for expiry, and renewed before they expire.

### Key management

1. Encryption keys must be generated by an approved key management service (KMS), hardware security module, or vetted cryptographic library using a cryptographically secure random number generator.
2. Keys must be stored in a KMS, HSM, or secrets manager. Keys and secrets must never be stored in source code, configuration files committed to version control, tickets, chat, or email.
3. Access to key management systems must be restricted to named key custodians and service identities that require it, following least privilege. Administrative access must require multi-factor authentication.
4. Data encryption keys should be protected by key encryption keys (envelope encryption) so that master keys never leave the KMS.
5. Customer-managed keys must be rotated at least every 365 days. Managed services with automatic rotation must have it enabled.
6. Keys must be rotated or revoked immediately when compromise is suspected or when a custodian with access to plaintext key material leaves {{org_name}}.
7. Use of keys must be logged, and key management logs must be retained in line with the Logging & Monitoring Policy.
8. Retired keys must be disabled and then destroyed once no data still depends on them. Destruction must follow a scheduled deletion window to prevent accidental loss of data.
9. An inventory of key types, their purpose, owner, location, and rotation schedule must be maintained and reviewed at least annually.

### Verification

1. Automated configuration checks must verify that storage encryption and TLS requirements are met, and deviations must be raised as findings for remediation.
2. Evidence of encryption settings and key rotation must be retained for audit.

## Exceptions

Exceptions, such as a legacy system that cannot support current TLS versions, must be requested in writing, include a risk assessment and compensating controls, and be approved by {{security_officer}}. Approved exceptions must have an expiry date of no more than 12 months and be recorded in the risk register.

## Enforcement

Violations of this policy may result in disciplinary action up to and including termination of employment or contract. Systems found to be non-compliant may be disconnected or have access restricted until remediated. Suspected key compromise must be handled as a security incident.

## Review

This policy is reviewed by {{security_officer}} at least annually, and whenever significant changes occur to cryptographic standards, infrastructure, or regulatory requirements. Changes must be approved and communicated to affected personnel.
