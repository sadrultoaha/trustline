# Information Security Policy

**Organization:** {{org_name}}
**Effective date:** {{effective_date}}
**Policy owner:** {{security_officer}}

## Purpose

This policy sets out how {{org_name}} protects the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of the information it holds, including information entrusted to it by customers, partners, and employees. It establishes the information security program, assigns accountability for it, and defines the framework of topic-specific policies that together describe how security is achieved in practice.

## Scope

This policy applies to:

- All employees, contractors, interns, and temporary workers ("personnel") of {{org_name}}.
- All information created, received, stored, or transmitted by {{org_name}}, in any format.
- All systems, devices, applications, networks, and cloud services owned, leased, or used on behalf of {{org_name}}, including personally owned devices used for work.
- Third parties who access {{org_name}} information or systems, to the extent defined in their contracts.

## Roles and responsibilities

- **Executive leadership** is accountable for the security program, must approve this policy, and must provide the resources needed to operate it.
- **Security officer ({{security_officer}})** is the designated owner of the information security program. The security officer maintains the policy set, coordinates risk assessments, tracks remediation, reports to leadership, and acts as the primary contact for security matters with customers, auditors, and regulators.
- **System and data owners** are responsible for ensuring the systems and data they own are classified, protected, and reviewed according to policy.
- **Managers** must ensure their teams complete required training and acknowledge policies, and must report suspected violations.
- **All personnel** must follow this policy and its supporting policies, protect the information they handle, and report suspected security incidents promptly.

## Policy statements

### Security program

1. {{org_name}} must maintain a documented information security program consisting of this policy, supporting topic-specific policies, standards, and procedures.
2. The program must be based on identified risks and must address, at a minimum: access control, asset and data management, change management, incident response, business continuity, vendor management, risk management, and acceptable use.
3. Security objectives must be defined at least annually and progress against them must be tracked.

### Roles and accountability

4. The security officer role must be formally assigned to a named individual, currently {{security_officer}}. If the role becomes vacant, leadership must assign an interim owner within 10 business days.
5. Security responsibilities for each role must be documented in job descriptions or a responsibility matrix, and must be communicated to the people holding those roles.
6. Personnel must not be assigned conflicting duties where the combination would allow a single person to commit and conceal an error or fraud, unless compensating controls are documented.

### Management oversight

7. The security officer must report on the state of the security program to executive leadership at least quarterly. Reports must cover open risks, incidents, audit findings, control status, and progress against objectives.
8. Leadership must review the program at least annually and record decisions, including risk acceptance and resource allocation, in meeting minutes or an equivalent record.

### Policy management

9. All security policies must be reviewed and approved at least once every 12 months, and whenever there is a significant change to the business, technology, threat environment, or legal obligations.
10. Each policy must record its owner, version, approval date, and next review date. Superseded versions must be retained for at least three years.
11. Policies must be published in a location accessible to all personnel.

### Policy acknowledgement

12. All personnel must acknowledge the information security policy and applicable supporting policies within 30 days of joining and again after each material policy revision.
13. At least {{policy_acceptance_threshold}}% of personnel must have a current acknowledgement of the policy set at all times. The security officer must monitor the acknowledgement rate and escalate shortfalls to managers.
14. Acknowledgement records must be retained for the duration of employment plus at least three years.

### Legal and regulatory obligations

15. {{org_name}} must maintain a register of legal, regulatory, and contractual security and privacy obligations that apply to it, including the jurisdiction, the requirement, and the owner responsible.
16. The register must be reviewed at least annually and whenever {{org_name}} enters a new market, launches a new product that processes personal data, or signs a contract with new security commitments.
17. Gaps between obligations and current practice must be recorded as risks and tracked to resolution.

### Information protection principles

18. Information must be classified according to its sensitivity and protected in proportion to that classification.
19. Access to information must be granted on the basis of business need and least privilege.
20. Security must be considered in the design of new systems, processes, and vendor relationships before they go live.
21. Suspected security incidents and policy violations must be reported to the security officer without undue delay.

## Exceptions

Exceptions to this policy or any supporting policy must be requested in writing, state the business justification and compensating controls, and be approved by the security officer. Exceptions that create high risk must also be approved by executive leadership. Every exception must have an expiry date of no more than 12 months and must be recorded in the risk register.

## Enforcement

Violations of this policy may result in disciplinary action up to and including termination of employment or contract, and may be reported to law enforcement where required. Compliance is monitored through control testing, audits, and automated monitoring.

## Review

This policy must be reviewed at least annually by the security officer and approved by executive leadership. The review must consider audit results, incidents, risk assessment outcomes, and changes in legal obligations.
