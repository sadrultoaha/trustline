# Privacy & Data Protection Policy

**Effective date:** {{effective_date}}
**Policy owner:** {{security_officer}}

## Purpose

{{org_name}} processes personal data about customers, users, employees, and partners. This policy sets the principles and procedures that ensure that personal data is handled lawfully and respectfully, in line with the EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) and other applicable privacy laws.

## Scope

This policy applies to all processing of personal data by {{org_name}}, whether as a controller (deciding why and how data is processed) or as a processor (processing data on behalf of customers). It covers all personnel, systems, and third parties that process personal data for {{org_name}}.

## Roles and responsibilities

- **Security Officer ({{security_officer}})** owns this policy and coordinates the privacy program, unless a separate Data Protection Officer (DPO) or privacy lead has been appointed, in which case that person is responsible for the privacy duties below.
- **Legal / privacy lead** maintains the register of legal and regulatory obligations, advises on lawful basis, and handles regulator contact.
- **Data owners and product teams** apply privacy by design, keep records of processing current, and carry out DPIAs.
- **All personnel** must process personal data only as needed for their role and report privacy concerns or incidents promptly.

## Policy statements

### Principles

Personal data must be:

1. Processed lawfully, fairly, and transparently.
2. Collected for specified, explicit, and legitimate purposes and not processed in ways incompatible with them.
3. Adequate, relevant, and limited to what is necessary.
4. Accurate and kept up to date.
5. Kept no longer than necessary, in line with the data retention schedule.
6. Protected by appropriate technical and organizational security measures.

### Lawful basis

1. Every processing activity must have a documented lawful basis: consent, contract, legal obligation, vital interests, public task, or legitimate interests.
2. Where legitimate interests is relied on, a legitimate interests assessment must be documented.
3. Where consent is used, it must be freely given, specific, informed, and unambiguous, recorded with a timestamp, and as easy to withdraw as to give.
4. Special-category data must be processed only where an additional condition under applicable law applies and must be classified as Restricted.

### Transparency

1. {{org_name}} must publish a privacy notice that explains what data is collected, purposes, lawful bases, recipients, international transfers, retention periods, and data subject rights.
2. The privacy notice must be reviewed at least annually and updated before any new processing begins that it does not cover.

### Data subject rights

1. {{org_name}} must support the rights of access, rectification, erasure, restriction, portability, objection, and the right not to be subject to solely automated decisions with significant effects.
2. Requests must be logged, the requester's identity verified, and a response provided within one month of receipt. This may be extended by up to two further months for complex or numerous requests, in which case the requester must be informed within the first month.
3. Where {{org_name}} acts as a processor, requests must be forwarded to the relevant customer without undue delay, and {{org_name}} must assist the customer in responding.

### Records of processing activities

1. A record of processing activities (RoPA) must be maintained, listing for each activity: purpose, categories of data subjects and personal data, recipients, international transfers and safeguards, retention period, and security measures.
2. The RoPA must be reviewed at least annually and updated when processing changes.

### Data protection impact assessments

1. A DPIA must be completed before starting processing that is likely to result in high risk to individuals, including large-scale processing of special-category data, systematic monitoring, profiling with significant effects, or new technologies such as AI applied to personal data.
2. DPIAs must describe the processing, assess necessity and proportionality, identify risks to individuals, and record mitigations. They must be approved by the privacy lead.
3. Where high risk remains after mitigation, the supervisory authority must be consulted before processing begins.

### Privacy by design and processors

1. New products and features must consider data minimization, pseudonymization, and default privacy settings during design.
2. Processors and sub-processors must be engaged only under a written data processing agreement, and a current sub-processor list must be made available to customers.
3. International transfers of personal data must rely on a valid transfer mechanism, such as an adequacy decision or standard contractual clauses.

### Personal data breaches

1. Suspected personal data breaches must be reported to {{security_officer}} immediately and handled under the Incident Response Plan.
2. Where {{org_name}} is the controller, breaches likely to result in risk to individuals must be notified to the competent supervisory authority within 72 hours of becoming aware of them. Where the risk is high, affected individuals must also be informed without undue delay.
3. Where {{org_name}} is a processor, affected customers must be notified without undue delay and within any contractual deadline.
4. All breaches, including those not notified, must be documented with facts, effects, and remedial actions.

### Legal and regulatory obligations

1. The legal / privacy lead must maintain a register of applicable privacy and security laws, regulations, and contractual commitments, and review it at least annually.

## Exceptions

Legal obligations in this policy cannot be waived. Exceptions to internal procedures must be approved by the privacy lead and {{security_officer}} and documented.

## Enforcement

Misuse of personal data may result in disciplinary action up to and including termination, and may expose individuals and {{org_name}} to legal penalties.

## Review

This policy is reviewed at least annually and whenever applicable privacy law or {{org_name}}'s processing activities change significantly.
